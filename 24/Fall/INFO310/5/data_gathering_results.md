Semi-Structured Interview: 

Overview: 

    Some questions, but mostly unguided conversation. 

    2 roommates, 1 bets often and the other less often.  

    Conducted interview with both at the same time to encourage conversation/dialogue. 

    Participants A, B 

Description of Participants: 

    Participant A is a Mechanical Engineering major at Drexel University. He is relatively new to the betting scene but has become a frequent bettor over the last year. He only bets of football games, often betting single bets on moneylines and multi-legged parlays. He bets on multiple platforms depending on the odds, but tends to gravitate towards DraftKings and FanDuel. He does not have any go-to sources of information to make bets on, rather mostly researches using social media to get recommendations from trusted sources, ESPN for some basic statistics, and his gut based on what he knows about each player/matchup. 

    Participant B recently graduated from Drexel University with a degree in Screenwriting and Playwriting, and now works at local theatre companies. He is new to the betting scene and bets infrequently, only when he feels confident about the bet. He bets a variety of sports, but primarily on football and often hockey. He only uses DraftKings to bet and is not concerned about finding the best odds. He only uses ESPN and Pro-football-reference for statistics reference and often calculates stats that he wants if he can’t find them there.  

Questions: 

1. How do you go about informing yourself on what bets to make? 

2. What sites do you use? 

3. What are some particular pain points of these sites that you use? 

4. Which of these websites have your favorite UI features? 

5. What features would you implement to improve these websites? 

 

Key results: 

    One stop shop: Both mentioned (unprompted) that they get annoyed that they must go to multiple sources for statistics, news, recommendations, etc.  

    Dedicated news feed 

    AI stat search 

    Clean UI: Both also mentioned that the UIs of existing websites are not ideal for what they need to make informed decisions. 

    Easy to find stats: They mentioned that it can be difficult to find the stats that they want to make better bets. One said that they like to see more relational data like rankings, grades, etc. To get an idea of where a particular player stands amongst the rest, and the other mentioned that they like to see trends among the stats to see exactly where a player stands among the league. 

    Convenient use: One of them mentioned that they use ESPN primarily because it is the most convenient and robust solution to find relevant live scores, lines, and basic information. Websites that have more of the information that they need to make informed bets like PFF or Pro-Football-Reference are not convenient to use because of their overwhelming UIs and bad search tools. 

    FanDuel=Draft Kings=ESPN Bet: All three of these sites (3 of, if not, the largest sportsbooks) have nearly identical UIs and features that makes it infuriating to switch platforms. 

Semi-structured interview 2: 

Overview: 

    Interviewed 2 people at the same time with individual responses from each. 

    One person bets often on football, baseball, and basketball, while the other bets infrequently on mostly baseball.  

    Participants C, D 

Description of participants: 

    Participant C: Civil engineering major at Drexel University. He is a frequent bettor that likes to bet primarily on football but will also bet on baseball and basketball during football’s offseason. He likes to bet on multiple platforms but mainly gravitates towards DraftKings and Fanduel. For statistics and research, he likes to use Linemate for basic stats and graphs on individual players and Statmuse for finding matchup-based statistics. Prefers to look at higher level statistics to better understand the nuances of player-player interactions that he feels confident about.  

    Participant D: Dual major in Business Analytics and Sports Business at Drexel University. Member of the University of Pennsylvania Baseball Team’s operations and analytics staff. Does not bet on baseball due to league rules but bets on Football and Basketball occasionally. Prefers to bet on Fanduel and Draftkings. For statistics and research, he prefers to use Baseball savant for its massive array of tracked stats, advanced metrics, and variety of graphs. Focuses on painting a picture of player-to-player interactions by using higher level statistics to understand the nuances of their interactions. For football and basketball, mainly bets on storylines and recommendations from trusted sources to bet low amounts on basic lines as he isn’t as familiar with those sports’ statistics as baseball. 

Questions/Answers: 

1. How do you go about informing yourself on what bets to make? 

C: Uses Linemate for trend graphs for individual players or teams for every sport. Also uses Statmuse for specific matchup based stats.  

D: Uses Baseball Savant for baseball research. Uses it to find high level metrics on pitchers, batters, and matchup statistics between them. “Finds the right stats to paint a picture of the matchups”. 

2. What stats do you want to see? 

C: Likes to see high level stats for both baseball and football. These can include barrel rates, BABIP, ISO, EPA/play, IAY/CMP, etc. 

D: Likes to observe high level pitcher and batter statistics to narrow down a better picture of the matchups between them. This includes a variety of rates, speed metrics, graphs on pitch placements/swings, etc.  

3. What are some particular pain points of these sites that you use? 

C: Likes to use Linemate but it doesn't have the high-level statistics that he wants. There are useful graphs that show player trends for every sport that he likes to bet on, but it only displays a limited amount based on base stats like passing yards, hits, strikeouts, etc instead of the higher-level ones described previously.  

D: Baseball savant is great for seeing the high-level stats that he wants but it was hard to get into using initially because of the sheer amount of data being thrown at your eyes at once. It is not friendly to new users and can be tough to navigate sometimes.  

4. Which of these websites have your favorite UI features? 

C: Linemate is very good at being able to switch between leagues. The mobile app has a convenient selector in the bottom corner in the screen that switches the interface to be more tailored to each sport.  

D: Baseball savant has convenient percentile rankings that make it easy to compare pitchers to batters in a “5th best pitcher against the 9th ranked batter in X category” kind of way. It also has charts that utilize a “hot/cold” visualization that shows blue for stats that are at the bottom of the league and red for stats that are at the top. It also has fantastic visualizations for pitchers that show a “radar” of where certain pitches land within the strike zone, and even allow users to click on them to see a video of the individual pitch.  

5. What features would you implement to improve these websites? 

C: Access to more advanced stats and graphs within the UI would be fantastic. Linemate has great visualizations that are easy to get to and customize for free, but it would be great to have some sort of switch that would allow more advanced stats to be shown. 

D: Filtering in baseball savant is not easy or not enough. By implementing better filtering or being able to create custom graphs that only show the data that you want to look at, it would reduce the headache of looking at so much data at once.  

Key Results: 

    High Level Statistics: Rate/Probability/tracked statistics are important to be able to paint a picture of what to bet on. Take a look at baseball statcast statistics to get an idea of what some of these advanced metrics can be (The idea can apply to all sports): https://www.mlb.com/glossary/statcast  

    Matchup statistics are important: Both mentioned the importance of being able to track statistics on matchups between players or between players/defenses. In baseball, it's imperative to be able to see a batter’s performance against a given pitcher or batters of a certain type against pitchers of a certain type to get a sense of how the game will go. In football, it's important to understand the matchup history of a given positional group against defenses of a certain type. For example, one might want to understand how Jalen Hurts performs against defenses that blitz a lot to bet the line on Hurts’ passing yards.  

    One stop shop: Both also mentioned sites that do not have any betting capabilities built into the app or website. Linemate seems to be the closest bossible solution to a website that provides everything that betters may want, however you cannot bet within the app, and it does not provide enough advanced metrics to be able to make informed decisions within the app. 

    Graphs/Visuals: Being able to see graphs like pitch movement, trend lines, or seeing the “hot/cold” features mentioned before help make more sense of the data for bettors.
