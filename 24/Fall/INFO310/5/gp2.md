# Description of Participants

    For the data gathering portion of our research, we were able to interview and observe 4 different participants. We will refer to them participants A, B, C, and D. We found that these participants would be good sources of valuable information for our study because they come from different backgrounds, study and work in different disciplines, and all have unique experiences and perspectives on sports betting. There are also a few reasons as to why we thought that 4 participants would be enought to gather sufficient data for our study. One of those reasons is that we had 2 weeks to fully complete our research, so based on the connections that we had as a group we were able to quickly gather a list of 4 people that we could gather some data points from and move on to the analysis portion of the research. We also thought that these four participants, based on their unique backgrounds in betting, would be able to offer us sufficiently unique data points to draw conclusions from. 
    Participant A is a senior studying mechanical engineering at Drexel University. They are 22 years old and from the Philadelphia area. Relatively new to the sports betting scene, they have only begun to sports bet within the last 6 months to 1 year. Football is their favorite sport to watch and bet on, however they are invested in all 4 major American sports in Baseball, Basketball and Hockey. When they do bet, they tend to bet $5-$10 on multi-legged parlays that could have large payouts or bet simple over/unders, or moneylines based on what players or games they are confident in. Typically they place these bets on the more popular Sportsbooks like DraftKings and FanDuel, whichever platforms has better odds for the bets they would like to place for that week. When setting out to research a given player or game to bet on, they typically start with social media apps like Instagram, TikTok, or Twitter to find out what trusted sources to them are betting on during a given week. These sources include NFL analysts, people from ProFootballFocus, or former players that understand the game better than he does. Occaisionally they will use ESPN for some basic statistics to fact-check those sources, however lots of the time they go based on a gut instinct since their bets are relatively low risk.
    Participant B is a recent graduate of Drexel University with a degree in Screenwriting and Playwriting. They currently work at several local theatre companies around the Philadelphia area, are 23 years old and grew up in several places throughout the United States including North Carolina, Oklahoma, and Tacoma, Washington. They are a more advanced bettor than participant A, having 2-3 years of betting experience under their belt, and bets a wider variety of sports including football, baseball, and hockey. Although they are more advanced, they are a more infrequent bettor, only betting when they are most confident about a certain prop, parlay, or moneline. They only use DraftKings to bet on, not looking at other Sportsbooks for better odds on the bets that he would like to place. Typically, these bets are mostly player props and moneylines, they do not like to bet on multi-legged parlays and the size of the bet is slightly larger than Participant A's at around $20-$50. They also oppose Participant A's method of research, opting for a more analytical approach based on statistics and matchup projections, tending to use ESPN and Pro-Football-Reference to analyze rates, matchup-based statistics, historical player numbers, and more. 
    Participant C is a 21 year old current Civil Engineering major at Drexel University and from the Philadelphia area. A veteran of the sports betting scene, they often place bets on several sports throughout the week on primarly football, baseball, and basketball. When placing a bet, they typically like to be well researched on a given player or team before betting. Linemate, Statmuse, and ESPN are their sources for statistics, trends, and favorable matchups. One Linemate they are able to see the sportbooks that have the most favorable odds for the bets that they have chosen, so they use a variety of sportsbooks to place bets with. Typically these bets are on the smaller side, around $5-$20 depending on the confidence in the bet, but they often place large multi-legged parlays, player props and moneylines that could have massive payouts. Participant C is definitely the most experienced and most frequent bettor of all participants in this study.
    Participant D is a 22 year old current dual major in Business Analytics and Sports Business at Drexel University. They have experience working in sports, working for the last 2 years in the Operations and Analytics staff for the University of Pennsylvania's Baseball team. As a result, for the last 2 seasons they are not allowed to sports bet according to their contract with Penn's baseball team. That being said, they were a frequent better before signing on to that job. Given that they work in baseball, their most frequent bet was on baseball games with football and basketball also being a frequent target to bet on. As opposed to Participant C, they are more like Participant A and B when betting on football or basketball, relying more on recommendations from others who know the game better than they do and only betting simple moneylines or over/unders. When it comes to baseball, however, they are definitely the most complex in terms of their statistical research out of all participants in this study. They use primarily Baseball Savant, the MLB's source of high-level baseball statistics to research pitcher to batter or pitcher to team matchups to bet on.T hey leverage Statcast statistics (advanced tracking metrics gathered using state-of-the art tracking technology), graphs on pitch placement, and stat projections in order to "paint a pitcure of each matchup".
